1 · The forum

What is Bitcointalk?

The place where early crypto projects were announced — before Twitter threads and Telegram groups took over.

Bitcointalk (bitcointalk.org) is one of the oldest and most important internet forums in cryptocurrency history. It launched in the early Bitcoin years and became the default public square for developers, miners, traders, and experimenters.

If someone created a new coin in 2013–2015, they usually posted an [ANN] — an announcement thread — on Bitcointalk. That thread was the project's homepage before the project had a real homepage: whitepaper links, wallet downloads, pool info, and argument threads all lived there.

In plain terms
What it was A message board for Bitcoin and "altcoins" (other cryptocurrencies)
Why it mattered Launching a coin there meant putting it in front of the people who mined, traded, and reviewed new projects
What an [ANN] is An announcement post: "here is our coin, here is how it works, here is how to get it"
Why FRSC is there Federal Reserve System Coin was announced on Bitcointalk in January 2014 — that post is still online

2 · The poster

Who is btcpay86?

The Bitcointalk account that launched FRSC — identified with Changpeng Zhao (CZ), later the founder of Binance.

The FRSC announcement was posted by a Bitcointalk user named btcpay86 (user ID 202137). In the community research around this coin, that account is identified as Changpeng Zhao — CZ — the person who would later found Binance, now one of the largest crypto exchanges in the world.

At the time of FRSC, CZ was not a household name. His public early-career story includes working at blockchain.info (today associated with Blockchain.com), helping people with wallets and exchange plumbing. That same kind of work shows up in btcpay86's Bitcointalk post history.

How the identification was made — step by step

Nobody "revealed" this. Anyone can rebuild the trail in about ten minutes, using nothing but public Bitcointalk pages. Here is the exact path, in order.

  1. 1

    Start at the coin's own announcement

    Open the 2014 [ANN] Federal Reserve Coin thread (topic 493871). The thread starter is the account that launched FRSC: btcpay86, user ID 202137. That is the only starting fact you need.

  2. 2

    Open that account's whole post history

    Go to the profile's post list (user 202137). You are not looking for a confession — you are reading a job description: wallets, exchange plumbing, user support, in the years before Binance existed.

  3. 3

    Check that he operated the coin, not just posted it

    Read his FRSC support thread of 24 February 2014: "Hi, guys, if you have some problems of FRSC, Please do not hesitate to tell me." He ships the wallet, the source code and the mining software, and answers users' problems. That is the behaviour of whoever built it.

  4. 4

    Search that history for blockchain.info

    Much of the account writes in Chinese, so turn on your browser's translate, then use Ctrl+F for blockchain.info. You land on posts like this reply (msg 6722268), where the account answers blockchain.info support questions and speaks about working there.

  5. 5

    Compare it with CZ's public timeline

    CZ's own public career story runs through blockchain.info (today Blockchain.com) and OKCoin before he founded Binance in 2017. Same years, same employer, same kind of hands-on work — from a Chinese-speaking account that also happened to launch a coin mocking the Federal Reserve. That overlap is the whole basis of the identification.

What the record proves: btcpay86 launched and operated FRSC, and discussed working at blockchain.info in 2014. What it does not contain: a signed confirmation of identity. Neither CZ nor Binance has commented. So this stays a community identification built on public sources — strong, but read it yourself and judge.

3 · The idea

What was Federal Reserve System Coin?

In layman terms — based on the original federalreservecoin.org homepage.

This was basically the homepage for an old cryptocurrency called FRSC — "Federal Reserve System Coin." Despite the name, nothing on the page suggests it was created or endorsed by the U.S. Federal Reserve. It was an independent crypto project using the Federal Reserve as its theme.

The basic concept

Idea What they were saying
Critique of the Fed The creators criticize the Federal Reserve and traditional monetary policy. They argue that central banks can create money, cause inflation, devalue currency, and use policies like quantitative easing ("printing").
Code instead of a central bank FRSC is presented as an alternative monetary system. Instead of a central bank deciding how much money exists, the supply of FRSC is governed by software / code.
Anniversary timing The project launched around the Federal Reserve's 100th anniversary era (site and ANN in early 2014, after the late-2013 QE taper news) — that anniversary gave them a narrative for the coin.
You could mine it Like early Bitcoin / Litecoin-style coins, FRSC could be mined. The page says it uses the Scrypt algorithm, produces roughly one block per minute, and has a predetermined schedule for reducing how many new coins are created.
User infrastructure The bottom of the original site was the practical toolkit: download the FRSC wallet, connect to mining pools, download mining software, and find exchanges where FRSC could be traded.

The joke, in one line

Central banks can inflate money by policy. FRSC's motto — WILL NOT BE DEVALUED COINS — was the punchline: a coin that claimed it wouldn't play that game, dedicated (ironically) to 100 years of the Federal Reserve.

Original site disclaimer: "This's Not The Fed's Official Website."

4 · When it happened

Timeline

From the Fed's taper news to the archive you can still open today.

  1. Dec 18, 2013

    The Fed announces QE taper

    The Federal Reserve says it will begin scaling back quantitative easing — the bond-buying program critics called money printing. That policy backdrop becomes part of FRSC's story on the original site.

  2. Jan 2014

    FRSC launches on Bitcointalk

    btcpay86 posts the [ANN] Federal Reserve Coin thread. The project is framed around the Federal Reserve's 100th anniversary and the "printing machine" critique. Motto: WILL NOT BE DEVALUED COINS.

  3. Feb 2014

    Original website is live

    federalreservecoin.org explains the concept, lists Scrypt / 1-minute blocks / emission schedule, and points users to wallet downloads, mining pools, mining software, and exchanges. Wayback captures from this period still exist.

  4. 2014 (ongoing)

    btcpay86 active on the forum

    The same account's broader Bitcointalk history includes wallet/exchange help and blockchain.info discussion — the trail researchers use when they identify btcpay86 as CZ.

  5. 2015–2020

    The project fades; the archive remains

    Mining interest dies down. The domain eventually lapses. About 31 Wayback Machine captures (through Nov 2020) preserve the seal, downloads, pools, and disclaimer.

  6. Later

    The story gets rediscovered — and relaunched

    People reconnect the ANN, the old website, and the btcpay86 → CZ identification. We bring $FRSC back on BNB Chain so the seal, the motto, and the critique have a living ticker again — not only archive pages.

5 · Why it still matters

Why the legacy must continue

The original chain died. The argument it carried did not.

Federal Reserve System Coin was never only a wallet download and a mining pool list. It was an early, public joke about power over money: who gets to create it, who pays when it is devalued, and what happens when that process is hidden behind institutions instead of open rules.

That joke was posted in 2014 by btcpay86 — CZ — years before Binance made him famous. Then the site went dark, the domain lapsed, and the record survived mostly in Bitcointalk threads and the Wayback Machine. If nobody carries it forward, one of crypto's strangest early artifacts stays a footnote.

Why continue it In plain terms
The critique aged well Debates about money printing, inflation, and who controls supply did not end in 2014. FRSC's theme is still readable today.
The origin is rare A Fed-themed coin announced by an account tied to CZ's early forum years is not generic meme lore — it is a specific historical object.
Archives are not enough Wayback captures preserve pages. A living ticker, seal, and community keep people actually reading the story.
The motto still lands WILL NOT BE DEVALUED COINS was the punchline. Continuing the legacy means keeping that line in circulation — not letting it die with a lapsed domain.
Culture needs memory Crypto moves fast and forgets faster. Continuing FRSC is a refusal to let early internet history get overwritten by whatever launches this week.

We do not continue FRSC to pretend we are the Federal Reserve. We continue it so the original joke, the paper trail, and the early-CZ artifact stay alive in public.

Quick answers

FAQ

Was FRSC made by the Federal Reserve?

No. The original site even said it was not the Fed's official website. It was an independent crypto project using the Fed as a theme and critique.

Who launched it?

Bitcointalk user btcpay86 (user 202137). Community research identifies that account as Changpeng Zhao (CZ), later founder of Binance. See the sources and decide for yourself.

What was the point of the coin?

To criticize central-bank money creation and offer a tongue-in-cheek alternative where supply rules lived in code — timed around the Fed's 100th anniversary and the QE taper news.

Could you mine it?

Yes. It was a Scrypt proof-of-work coin with about one block per minute and a published emission schedule. The old site linked wallets, pools, and miners.

Why the “Live Fed desk”?

Because the original point was never only historical. The desk pulls live Fed rates, policy releases, and FRED charts so you can see the same institution still setting money conditions by committee — the critique FRSC made in 2014.

Why relaunch FRSC at all?

So the legacy does not die in the archive. The original critique, seal, and btcpay86 / CZ paper trail deserve a living ticker and community — not only Wayback captures.

What is the purpose of the token you launched?

Cultural continuity. $FRSC on BNB Chain exists to keep the 2014 Federal Reserve System Coin story circulating: the Fed satire, the motto, and the public sources. It is a community legacy token, not an official CZ/Binance/Fed product, and not the original scrypt chain.
