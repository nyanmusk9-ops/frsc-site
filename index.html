Case file · Opening brief

What is Federal Reserve Coin?

Not a product pitch. An artifact from early crypto culture — and a paper trail worth reading.

A joke coin timed to the Fed's 100th birthday. The punchline outlived the chain.

In January 2014, while the Federal Reserve marked a century of existence and began tapering quantitative easing, a Bitcointalk user named btcpay86 announced Federal Reserve Coin (FRSC): a virtual currency dedicated to "printing," complete with a seal, a motto, and a disclaimer that it was not the Fed's official website.

The coin itself nearly vanished. The idea did not. This site documents the concept, the 2014 archive, and the public forum record that researchers use when they ask whether btcpay86 was Changpeng Zhao (CZ) — years before Binance.

Thesis

The concept: mocking the printer

FRSC was satire dressed as a coin — central banking as a meme, before "meme coin" was a category.

The Federal Reserve's first century was also the age of QE: emergency policy that critics nicknamed the printing machine. On December 18, 2013, the Fed announced it would taper bond purchases. A month later — on the institution's 100th birthday window — FRSC appeared as a tongue-in-cheek "virtual currency" so holders could "feel the true meaning of the Federal Reserve system."

The joke had structure. A seal. A motto in capitals. Emission rules that deliberately echoed monetary tinkering. A homepage that insisted it was unofficial. It was not trying to be the next Litecoin clone; it was trying to be a punchline with a blockchain.

Idea How FRSC played it
Centennial timing Launched against the Fed's 100-year mark — dedication as branding
QE / "printing" Site copy framed the coin as a response to five years of easy money
Credibility theater Institutional seal + formal tone + "This's Not The Fed's Official Website"
Hard money irony Motto: WILL NOT BE DEVALUED COINS — the opposite of what the joke accused the Fed of doing
Infinite mining Original chain: infinite mining time — the printer never fully shuts off

The concept is the product. Everything else — chain, domain, ticker revival — is costume around that joke.

Historical record

Timeline of the artifact

From taper announcement to archive dust — then the story resurfaces.

  1. Dec 2013

    The Fed blinks

    The Fed announces the taper of quantitative easing. "Printing machine" policy finally has an exit narrative — and crypto culture has fresh material.

  2. Jan 2014

    FRSC is announced

    btcpay86 posts the [ANN] Federal Reserve Coin thread on Bitcointalk. A site goes up at federalreservecoin.org. Scrypt, one-minute blocks, a seal, a motto.

  3. 2014

    The account keeps posting

    Away from the ANN thread, user 202137's history looks like infrastructure work: wallets, exchanges, and public answers about blockchain.info — the company CZ has said he worked at before founding Binance.

  4. 2015–2024

    The missing years

    Interest in the chain fades. The domain lapses. Roughly 31 Wayback captures remain (Jan 2014–Nov 2020): downloads, pools, the unofficial disclaimer. The joke survives as residue.

  5. Later

    The story returns

    Researchers and communities rediscover the ANN, the seal, and the btcpay86 trail. The ticker and imagery get new life in crypto culture — as lore first, market instrument second. This file is about that lore.

Attribution file

btcpay86 and the CZ question

The interesting claim is not "buy this." It is: who posted the joke — and does the public record point to CZ?

Changpeng Zhao is publicly associated with an early career at blockchain.info / Blockchain.com, then with building Binance. Separately, Bitcointalk user 202137 (display name btcpay86) launched FRSC and left a trail of posts that look like hands-on crypto ops work in the same era.

This portal does not treat identity as proven. It lays out why people make the connection — and links the primary sources so you can test it.

Public information

Questions about the file

Concept, attribution, and scope — not a trading desk FAQ.

What was Federal Reserve Coin actually about?

Satire of central banking and QE, launched as a Bitcointalk coin around the Fed's 100th birthday. The seal, motto, and "unofficial" disclaimer were part of the joke's costume.

Was btcpay86 really CZ?

This portal does not assert official identity. It points to Bitcointalk user 202137's public posts — including blockchain.info discussion — which researchers compare to CZ's known early career. Read the receipts and decide.

Is this site claiming CZ or Binance endorse FRSC?

No. There is no claimed affiliation or endorsement by CZ, Binance, BNB Chain, blockchain.info / Blockchain.com, or the Federal Reserve System.

Is today's $FRSC the same as the 2014 coin?

No. The 2014 project was a scrypt proof-of-work chain. Modern tickers reuse the name, seal, and story. Technically and legally they are not the same network.

Why isn't this page full of tokenomics and buy buttons?

Because the durable object is the concept and the paper trail. Market mechanics change; the 2014 ANN and Wayback archive do not. See the appendix for a short contemporary note.

Will FRSC be devalued?

Per the 2014 homepage, in capitals: WILL NOT BE DEVALUED COINS.
